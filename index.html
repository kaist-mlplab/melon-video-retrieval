MELON: A Large-Scale Dataset for Multi-Event Text-to-Long-Video Retrieval

A text-to-video retrieval dataset of 12,000 long-form videos with 42,526 annotated events, plus a multi-event aware (MEA) training loss

arXiv 2026

Chan Hur1,*, SeungWoo Song2,*, Jeong-hun Hong3,*, Won Jun Oh2, Hyeyoung Park3, KyungTae Lim2

1ETRI 2KAIST 3Kyungpook National University

*Equal contribution

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

Existing text-video retrieval datasets primarily consist of short-form clips containing a single dominant event. While suitable for measuring basic vision-language alignment, they are limited in capturing real-world retrieval scenarios, where long-form videos naturally contain multiple semantically distinct events and a single text query may correspond to several non-contiguous temporal segments. To bridge this gap, we introduce MELON, the first large-scale dataset designed to extend text-video retrieval to long-form videos featuring complex, multi-event structures. MELON explicitly annotates multiple event intervals per video along with their corresponding textual descriptions, enabling both training and evaluation of multi-event understanding in long, untrimmed videos. In addition, we propose a multi-event aware loss that encourages models to differentiate between full-event and partial-event matches, yielding substantial improvements in retrieval accuracy. Together, the MELON dataset and our proposed loss establish a robust foundation for expanding text-to-video retrieval to complex long-form scenarios and provide a more realistic evaluation setting for future research in the field.

Dataset Construction and MEA Loss

MELON (Multi-Event Text-to-Long Video Retrieval) curates long-form videos from six recent video-language benchmarks that provide fine-grained timestamps. Every video is at least 2 minutes long and is paired with a query made of 3 to 5 non-overlapping event descriptions:

  1. Collection and refinement. Videos shorter than 2 minutes and sparse or misaligned annotations are discarded. Dense temporal captions are used directly; timestamped question–answer pairs are converted into event captions by an LLM.
  2. Quality filtering. T2T filtering drops videos whose consecutive descriptions are too similar (similarity > 0.70), removing 14.2% of videos; V2T filtering removes timestamps whose video–text similarity is below a dataset-specific threshold (about 0.12–0.18), removing about 16.6% of event segments.
  3. Multi-event summary. Qwen3-235B-A22B condenses the remaining segments into K = 3–5 salient events per video, each with a theme, description, and timestamp.
  4. Test-set validation. A multimodal LMM (Gemini) checks description–timestamp–video consistency, followed by human review.

Multi-event aware (MEA) loss. Standard contrastive (InfoNCE) training over-rewards videos that match only some of the queried events (the partial event matching problem). MEA builds partial queries by dropping events from the full query and adds two hinge terms to the contrastive loss: a push term that keeps each partial query less similar to the video than the full query by an adaptive margin (the previous step's average gap, capped at half the gap to the hardest negative), and a pull term that keeps partial queries more similar to the video than the hardest in-batch negative. The total objective is the standard contrastive loss plus a weighted MEA loss; it is plug-and-play and needs no temporal segmentation at inference.

Main Results

  • Scale and length. MELON contains 12,000 long-form videos (8,400 train / 3,600 test) and 42,526 annotated events across ten themes. Videos average 12.4 minutes, compared with at most 145 seconds for the prior text-video retrieval datasets compared in the paper (e.g., 2 minutes for ActivityNet-Cap, 10 seconds for MSR-VTT); over 75% of videos span 2–10 minutes and 5.0% exceed 60 minutes.
  • MEA improves every model. Adding the MEA loss raises Recall and Rsum for all seven CLIP-based retrievers in both directions. The largest text-to-video R@1 gain is for PAU (48.5 → 55.4); X-CLIP has the largest combined gain (Rsum +13.0 text-to-video and +17.3 video-to-text; video-to-text R@1 50.6 → 58.1); TempMe + MEA reaches the best text-to-video R@1 of 59.2.
  • The test set penalizes partial matches. For five-event queries, CLIP4Clip + MEA reaches 57.9% R@1 with the full multi-event query, but only 7.7% average R@1 when each single event is used as the query.
  • Both MEA terms are needed. Using only the push term drops text-to-video R@1 to 45.2 (baseline 52.7); the full MEA loss gives 55.1 (+2.4).
  • Generalizes beyond MELON. With a TempMe backbone, MEA gives R@1 gains of up to +2.4 on ActivityNet-Cap and +3.2 on DiDeMo. The one-to-many multi-event method MeVTR reaches only 41.4 text-to-video R@1 on MELON.
